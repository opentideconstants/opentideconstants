Sources

Every dataset OTC incorporates, what OTC takes from each, and how each one is credited. Each source has its own page with its licence, attribution, access, update cadence and coverage.

How sources rank at one location

When more than one source covers the same location, the station's recommended set is chosen in this order:

  1. Official constants: harmonic constants published by the agency responsible for the gauge (for example NOAA, Kartverket).
  2. Gauge fit: constants OTC fits from a gauge record (GESLA).
  3. Model constants: constants from a global or regional tide model, labelled as model constants. EOT20 and FES2022 are the model sources.

Every set stays in the release files, not only the recommended one, so you can choose another. A source must also pass the per-source convention check; a source that fails is not used in that release. The validation table shows how each set scores against official predictions.

The sources

OTC combines fifteen open sources. For each one, the licence was read and the access path was checked on 2026-10-07 (LINZ and SHOM REFMAR on 2026-10-08). The coverage figures on each source's page are from those checks.

Most give gauge records, from which OTC fits its own constants. NOAA CO-OPS, Kartverket and LINZ publish official constants. EOT20 and FES2022 are global tide models: their constants are model constants, interpolated to station points, labelled as model constants in every file, and ranked below official constants and gauge fits.

Method

How a release is built, from the source records to the published files.

Fitting

  • Basis: least squares on the reference predictor's own equation, h(t) = Z0 + c·(t − t̄) + Σ fk·(ak cos Xk + bk sin Xk), where Xk = speedk·t + (V0+u)k. Speeds, V0+u and f come from the same tables the predictor uses. The fitted constants therefore plug into the predictor exactly, and the convention mismatches on the Why page cannot arise.
  • Span: the last 19 years of good data (one nodal cycle is 18.61 years). A shorter record is fitted whole; records under 1 year are not used, and records of 1–3 years are flagged as short. A linear trend is fitted alongside, so sea-level rise does not leak into the annual constituent.
  • Quality control before fitting: null values, values flagged as not usable, and duplicate time stamps are dropped. Sub-hourly records are reduced to one value per hour. Spikes are removed against a rolling median of the residual, and the fit is repeated.
  • Uncertainty: for each constituent, the larger of the residual noise in its frequency band and half the difference between fits to the two halves of the span.
  • Determinism: fixed random seeds, sorted inputs, fixed output precision. Two builds from the same inputs must give byte-identical files.

Time-base checks

Every record that a build might use is checked for a wrong or undeclared clock. The metadata cannot be trusted for this: in GESLA-4.1, 6,681 of 6,682 records declare a time zone offset of 0, including records that are not UTC.

  1. Comparators, in order of preference: official predictions near the gauge; NOAA harmonic constants for NOAA records; and other records at the same gauge from a different contributor, after their own correction.
  2. Measurements: the monthly time lag at 1-min resolution, the lag per constituent, amplitude ratios, a direct test of instantaneous against averaged sampling, and the missing hour on daylight-saving change days.
  3. Verdict classes, each with a fixed correction: UTC instantaneous; UTC hourly mean stamped at the start of the hour (shift +30 min and correct the amplitudes); UTC plus a whole number of hours; local time with daylight saving; a step at a date; isolated bad months.
  4. Pass rule: after the correction, the residual M2 lag must be within ±3 min in every month used. A record that matches no class is not used as it is.
  5. Positive controls on every build: pinned GESLA-4.0 WSV files must come out as local time with daylight saving; a record shifted by +60 min must be detected; an hourly-averaged copy must be detected. If a control fails, the build stops.

Broken-record checks

A record is excluded, and listed with its numbers, when its main constituents are not stable from year to year or between the two halves of the span, when it does not correlate with a nearby gauge that correlates with its own neighbours, or when its values are quantised or out of range. The Hirtshals record (no tide in the input) is the reference case. The thresholds are calibrated on the data and published with each release.

Record selection

When several records exist at one gauge, the build picks one by fixed rules, in this order: the most good data in the 19-year span; recent records before records that ended long ago; the gauge operator's own record before a re-distribution of it; instantaneous before averaged; sub-hourly before hourly; then the lowest file id. When two eligible records disagree beyond their uncertainty, the record with the lower error against an official reference is chosen. Every choice and its reason is recorded.

Constituent selection

  1. Candidates: every constituent the predictor's tables define: NOAA's 37 plus EPS2, MKS2, SIG1, N4, S3, 2MK5, 2MO5 and 2MS6, in a fixed priority order.
  2. Record length (Rayleigh rule): a candidate is kept only when the record is long enough to separate it from every higher-priority constituent already kept.
  3. Signal to noise: after a first fit, a constituent is kept only when its amplitude is at least twice the residual noise in its frequency band. Then the fit is repeated.
  4. Long-period constituents (SA, SSA, MM, MF, MSF) are left out of the published set at river and lake gauges, and where SA is 0.25 m or more, because official predictions do not contain river and seasonal discharge signals.
  5. Non-tidal daily cycles (S1, S3, S4) are left out at river and lake gauges, and where they are larger than both M2 and K1.

At the validation stations this keeps 41–45 constituents for 19-year records and 23–28 for records of 1.5–2.5 years. Every constituent in a release records why it was kept or dropped.

Per-source convention check

Every source is compared on every build with an independent comparator at the same gauges: our own GESLA fit, predicted with the reference predictor. The pass rule is that the median M2, S2, K1 and O1 differences are within 1 cm and 2° over at least 3 overlapping gauges. A source that fails is not published in that release until its declared convention is fixed. A source with no overlapping gauge cannot be added.

Automation

  • Watchers check every source weekly and produce a list of changed records.
  • Only changed sets are rebuilt. A new GESLA release, a change in the predictor's tables, or a format change triggers a full rebuild.
  • Builds run on GitHub Actions, so the build history is public.
  • After a release, the webcaltides app receives the new release through an automated, pinned update that merges only when its own checks pass.

The release gate (no person in the loop)

Every judgement in a build is made in three tiers, and nothing waits for a person:

  1. Fixed rules decide every case they can classify: pass, fail or fall back.
  2. One batched automated review per build handles the cases the rules cannot classify. Each answer is either accept or fall back, with a reason that cites the evidence. Answers are stored in a public table keyed by the evidence, and reused while the evidence is unchanged, so a rebuild is reproducible.
  3. Fallback: anything still undecided keeps the station's data from the last release, and the reason is listed in the release's changes on Downloads.

A release is published only when every whole-release gate passes, including the round-trip checks of the converters and the uploads. If any gate fails, nothing is published, the last good release stays live, and an alert opens a public issue. Licence and publication policy stay with the project owner and never block a build.

Conventions

A harmonic constant is only meaningful together with its convention. Every OTC constant set points to a declared convention record, and a converter never guesses one.

Phase reference

  • OTC publishes Greenwich phase lags g in degrees, with time in UTC. This is NOAA's phase_GMT convention.
  • A source that gives phases in a local time zone is converted to Greenwich by its adapter, and the conversion is recorded. Example: Kartverket's API gives phases with utcoffset="+01:00".
  • The convention record states the reference as greenwich_utc, or local with the offset, for the source data before conversion.

Astronomical arguments and nodal corrections

  • The reference predictor uses the XTide/NOAA (Schureman) conventions. The equilibrium arguments V0+u and the node factors f are applied at prediction time, per year, from the predictor's tables.
  • Constants fitted by OTC are therefore free of nodal modulation: the fit uses the same f and u that the predictor applies again. Applying them twice, or not at all, is the error behind the OO1 behaviour described on the Why page.
  • The convention record names the V0 model (for example schureman_tcd) and the nodal handling (f_u_at_prediction with formula ids, or none).
  • Fitted constants are tied to the predictor's tables by construction. The build records the tables' SHA-256, and a change to the tables triggers a full rebuild.

Constituents that need care

SA (solar annual)
NOAA and the reference predictor use V = h. The Doodson Sa (056 554) is h − p1, which differs by about 77°. TICON-3 SA phases showed this offset (+76° measured); TICON-4 SA phases follow V = h.
M1
Several incompatible definitions exist (Doodson 155 555, 155 655 and the Schureman composite). OTC uses the Schureman composite, as NOAA does. TICON-4 M1 is about −105° from NOAA's M1, and is not used.
OO1
Must be fitted with the same nodal corrections that are applied at prediction time. TICON-4 OO1 on short records behaves as if no effective nodal correction was applied.
EPS2, MKS2
NOAA does not publish them, so they cannot be cross-checked against NOAA.

Units

Amplitudes in metres (tidal-current amplitudes in metres per second), phases in degrees, speeds in degrees per hour, times in UTC. Field names are stable within a major format_version; see Data format.

The convention table

The full constituent table (Doodson numbers, speeds, node-factor formula ids and the IHO standard name where one exists) is in every release, in the constituents field of the JSON file and in OTC_{YYYYMMDD}.meta.json.

Validation

Every release publishes, for every station with an official reference, how well its constants reproduce the official predictions.

How accuracy is measured

  1. The constants are run through the reference predictor (the webcaltides engine, which uses the XTide/NOAA Schureman conventions) to produce high and low water events.
  2. The events are matched with the official predicted events of a reference source near the station (for example NOAA, BSH, Kartverket).
  3. For each station and each time window, the build reports:
    • time error: mean absolute error of matched event times (minutes), 95th percentile and bias;
    • height error: mean absolute error of event heights after removing the mean offset, and the tidal range error;
    • missed and extra events against the reference;
    • the reference source and its distance from the station;
    • the same numbers for the previous release.
  4. Control: the predictor run on NOAA's own constants must reproduce NOAA's predictions. It does so at 0.25–0.27 min mean time error at NOAA reference stations. This is the floor the predictor itself sets.

What the results show

  • With the same 9 constituents and a correct time base, our constants and TICON-4's predict equally well. The gains come from three things: correct time bases (Germany), the full constituent set in the predictor's own convention, and fitting the last 19 years instead of the whole record.
  • Fitting the whole record instead of the last 19 years lost most of the gain: San Francisco 3.2 → 8.1 min, Key West 3.5 → 5.7 min.
  • Short records stay weak whatever the method: the Gulf of Mexico stations, with 1.5–2.5-year records, remain at 12–31 min.
  • Missed plus extra events, summed over the three windows, were no higher than TICON-4's at every station; Leeville went from 14 to 2.

Data format

The release files and where to download them are on Downloads.

Fields

The JSON document has six top-level fields: format_version, release, conventions, licences, constituents and stations.

station
A stable station_id in the OTC namespace (never changed, never reused), name, country, position, IANA time zone, type (reference or subordinate), aliases in other systems (NOAA, GESLA, TICON, XTide, Kartverket, Slackwater), and the recommended_set_id. An alias id belongs to one station only in a release. A removed station stays as a tombstone with its reason.
constant set
One per source record: source, source type (official, gauge or model), quantity (water_level or current), record id and version, record span, datum, convention_id, licence_id, QC status and flags, the constituents, and provenance. A station can have several sets; one is recommended by a published rule.
constituent
OTC canonical name and the source's own name, Doodson number, speed (°/h), amplitude (m), Greenwich phase (°), uncertainties, and why it was kept. Dropped constituents are listed with the reason. Names are at most 15 characters.
constituent table
One row per constituent name and constituent-table version in the release: Doodson number, speed (°/h) and nodal formula id.
current bin
Only in a set with quantity current, which has no water-level constituents. One per depth bin: bin number, depth (m) and how it is measured, major-axis azimuth (° true), mean flood and ebb directions, and per constituent the speed (°/h), major- and minor-axis amplitude (m/s) and Greenwich phase (°). The station's default_current_bin names the bin to show first.
subordinate offsets
Reference station, time offsets at high and low water (minutes), height offsets, and whether they are ratios or additive, as NOAA gives them. A subordinate current station has current_offsets instead, one per bin: reference station and bin, mean flood and ebb directions, time adjustments for maximum flood, slack before ebb, maximum ebb and slack before flood (minutes), and flood and ebb amplitude ratios.
convention
Phase reference, V0 model, nodal handling, constituent-table version, and the result of the per-source check that confirmed it. See Conventions.
validation
Per station and window: time mean absolute error, 95th percentile, bias, height error, range error, missed and extra events, the reference source and its distance, and the same numbers for the previous release. See Validation.
licence
SPDX id (or a LicenseRef- id), provider, required citation, and the assembled attribution text. See Licence and attribution.

JSON Schema

  • otc-0.4.schema.json: the JSON Schema (draft 2020-12) for format_version 0.4. Each line of the .jsonl file validates against #/$defs/station, and the .meta.json file against #/$defs/meta.
  • otc-0.3.schema.json, otc-0.2.schema.json and otc-0.1.schema.json: the earlier versions, for format_version 0.3, 0.2 and 0.1. Kept for reference. 0.3 adds alias systems for the gauge and official sources (linz, shom, pegelonline, rws, mi, dmi, smhi, fmi, uhslc). 0.4 adds tidal-current constants (current_bins), current offsets and default_current_bin.
  • example.json: a small example document with illustrative values (not data). It has one tide station with two constant sets, one tombstone, one current station (NOAA ACT1616, with five of its constituents converted from NOAA's published values) and one subordinate current station.

The format version is separate from the release date. A new field is a minor change; a removed or renamed field, or a changed unit or convention, is a major change.

Converters

Converters, in both directions, with a lossless round trip as a release gate: TICON CSV, XTide TCD (through libtcd), and NOAA harmonic-constant JSON. The reference predictor publishes conformance vectors (expected high and low water times and heights at named stations and dates) so that other predictors can check their results.
