Data format

The fields of a release, its JSON Schema and the converters. The files and where to download them are on Downloads.

Fields

The JSON document has six top-level fields: format_version, release, conventions, licences, constituents and stations.

station
A stable station_id in the OTC namespace (never changed, never reused), name, country, position, IANA time zone, type (reference or subordinate), aliases in other systems (NOAA, GESLA, TICON, XTide, Kartverket, Slackwater), and the recommended_set_id. An alias id belongs to one station only in a release. A station id that is not used any more stays as a tombstone: removed_reason is merged or duplicate, and merged_into names the station that holds its records. A station is never removed because its records are short, broken or from a source that failed a check. A reference station has at least one constant set, and its recommended_set_id is null only when every set is no_constants. A subordinate station has constant sets, subordinate_offsets or current_offsets.
constant set
One per source record: source, source type (official, gauge or model), quantity (water_level or current), record id and version, record span (start, end and hours of good data, good_hours, required on every set fitted from a gauge record), datum, convention_id, licence_id, QC status and flags, the constituents, and provenance. Every record stays listed. A station can have several sets; one is recommended by a published rule. Only sets of the same quantity compete for it (water level or current). A short record never outranks a set at the same station that has constants and more hours of good data, and a set with no constants is never recommended.
QC status and flags
qc_status is ok (constants, no flags), flagged (constants, and qc_flags says what to know) or no_constants (the set is listed, but no constants were fitted; no_constants_reason is too_short or fit_unusable, and the set has at least one flag). No status marks a record as removed: every record stays listed. Every flag has a reason (required): time_base, broken_record, short_record, carried_over, microtidal, non_tidal_signal or sibling_disagreement. A carried_over set keeps the constants of an earlier release, named in provenance.carried_from_release, because its source failed a check in this build.
provenance
Where the set came from and what was done to it. It is a closed list of keys, so no key can carry a status: qc_status is the only status of a set. For every source: adapter_version, input_sha256, tcd_sha256, selection_reason, decision (the release gate's tier, outcome, rule and reason), carried_from_release, and build: the build-only fields build_commit, built_at, fetched_at, run_id and tool_versions, which change on every rebuild, so the release content digest leaves the whole object out. Deprecated: build_commit at the top (use build.build_commit) and the free-text time_base. Source-specific keys are listed on the source pages: JMA (owner, redistribution, phase_reference_longitude_deg, jma_station_code, analysis_years, source_constituents_not_published) and LINZ (member, position, header_dropped). A new key is added to the schema before a writer uses it.
time-base annotation
Required on every set fitted from a gauge record (time_base): the code (verified, corrected, unverified or disputed), a reason for people, the declared and published time base, the correction from and to (only when corrected), any months left out (months_dropped, a field, not a class), the build that decided it (decided_in, and optionally its decided_build_commit), and the evidence: per comparison, the comparator kind and id, its distance (km), the measured lag in minutes (the record's time stamp of an event minus the comparator's time of it, so an hourly mean stamped at the start of its hour is about −30), and an optional detail for people. A time base is a class such as utc_instant, utc_mean_start60, utc_plus_1, local_dst:Europe/Oslo or step:2010-03-01. An unverified or disputed time base also has a time_base flag. See Time-base checks.
broken record
record_issues, one per problem: datum_step, flatline, spikes, gaps, clock_step, quantised, out_of_range, unstable_constituents, neighbour_disagreement or no_tide, with where it is and why. fit_segment names the part the constants are fitted to; it is absent when the whole record is fitted. Comes with a broken_record flag. See Broken-record checks.
short record
A short_record flag, the record length (record_span.good_hours), the constituents left out in dropped_constituents (at least one; a Rayleigh drop names the constituent it cannot be separated from, not_separable_from), and expected_accuracy: the length long records were cut to, how many, and the expected amplitude and phase error or the expected time and height error. A record with less than about 15 days of good data (release.fit_min_good_hours) is no_constants with too_short. See Short records.
constituent
OTC canonical name and the source's own name, Doodson number, speed (°/h), amplitude (m), Greenwich phase (°), uncertainties, and why it was kept. Dropped constituents are listed with the reason. Names are at most 15 characters.
constituent table
One row per constituent name and constituent-table version in the release: Doodson number, speed (°/h) and nodal formula id.
current bin
Only in a set with quantity current, which has no water-level constituents. One per depth bin: bin number, depth (m) and how it is measured (below_surface, below_chart_datum or unknown, from NOAA's S, B and U), major-axis azimuth (° true), mean flood and ebb directions, the mean current along the major and minor axes (m/s, signed), and per constituent the speed (°/h), major- and minor-axis amplitude (m/s) and Greenwich phase (°). The station's default_current_bin names the bin to show first.
subordinate offsets
Reference station, time offsets at high and low water (minutes), height offsets, and whether they are ratios or additive, as NOAA gives them. A subordinate current station has current_offsets instead, one per bin: reference station and bin, mean flood and ebb directions, time adjustments for maximum flood, slack before ebb, maximum ebb and slack before flood (minutes), and flood and ebb amplitude ratios.
convention
Phase reference, V0 model, nodal handling, constituent-table version, and the result of the per-source check that confirmed it. See Conventions.
validation
Per station and window: time mean absolute error, 95th percentile, bias, height error, range error, missed and extra events, the reference source and its distance, and the same numbers for the previous release. See Validation.
release
The datestamp, build time, upstream source versions, build commit and changelog URL, and concept_doi: the one DOI for all versions. Cite a release by the concept DOI and its datestamp. fit_min_good_hours is the build's minimum hours of good data for a fit. release.doi is always null: a Zenodo version DOI is made after the files are fixed, so it is only in OTC_index.json and the latest pointers (zenodo_version_doi). min_reader_version is the oldest format version a reader must understand to use the release safely.
release index and latest pointers
OTC_index.json lists every release, newest first, in releases. OTC_latest.json and OTC_latest-f{MAJOR}.json are one entry each. An entry has the datestamp, format_version, min_reader_version, concept_doi, zenodo_version_doi (or null), content_sha256, and the name, URL, size and SHA-256 of each file. Optional: created, changes, source_versions, changelog_url and zenodo_material (pipeline only; true when the release gets its own Zenodo version at publish). A reader checks min_reader_version before it downloads a release.
licence
SPDX id (or a LicenseRef- id), provider, required citation, and the assembled attribution text. See Licence and attribution.

JSON Schema

  • otc-0.6.schema.json: the JSON Schema (draft 2020-12) for format_version 0.6. Each line of the .jsonl file validates against #/$defs/station, and the .meta.json file against #/$defs/meta.
  • otc-index-0.6.schema.json: the JSON Schema for OTC_index.json. Each latest pointer validates against #/$defs/release_info. index-example.json is an example.
  • otc-0.5.schema.json, otc-0.4.schema.json, otc-0.3.schema.json, otc-0.2.schema.json and otc-0.1.schema.json: the earlier versions, for format_version 0.5, 0.4, 0.3, 0.2 and 0.1. Kept for reference. 0.3 adds alias systems for the gauge and official sources (linz, shom, pegelonline, rws, mi, dmi, smhi, fmi, uhslc). 0.4 adds tidal-current constants (current_bins), current offsets and default_current_bin. 0.5 adds the mean current of a bin (mean_major_ms, mean_minor_ms) and the depth type below_chart_datum for NOAA's B, which 0.4 wrongly called above_bottom.
  • What 0.6 changes: every record stays listed, with annotations. qc_status is ok, flagged or no_constants; excluded is not valid in 0.6. 0.6 adds the time-base annotation, record issues and fit segment, the short-record fields (good_hours, not_separable_from, expected_accuracy), no_constants_reason, carried-over sets and min_reader_version, and makes a tombstone a merge or a duplicate. Deprecated in 0.6 and still valid for one version: qc_status accepted (read as ok, or flagged when it has flags) and fallback (read as a carried-over set), qc_flags[].verdict (0.6 replaces it with reason) and release.doi (always null). 0.6 replaces the free-text provenance.time_base with the set's time_base: verdict becomes published, correction becomes correction from and to, and comparators become evidence. For writers: the build's time-base audit uses short internal codes for its comparators, and they map to comparator_kind as: b (official predictions) → official_predictions, c (official harmonic constants) → official_constants, a (another record at the gauge) → sibling_record, inherit (the provider's other records) → provider, and w (the weak checks) → model (EOT20), served_constants or dst_selftest, by the check that gave the verdict.
  • example.json: a small example document with illustrative values (not data). It has one tide station with two constant sets, one tombstone, one current station (NOAA ACT1616, with five of its constituents and its mean current converted from NOAA's published values), one subordinate current station, and one station with five GESLA records: a corrected time base, a broken record fitted on its usable segment, a short record with a disputed time base, a record too short for constants, and a broken record with no usable segment.

The format version is separate from the release date. A new optional field is a minor change. A removed or renamed field, or a changed unit or convention, is a major change. A change to an enum is a minor change for the schema, but it can change what a reader does with a set, so it raises min_reader_version: 0.6 sets it to 0.6, because a 0.5 reader filters out only excluded sets and would treat a no_constants set as usable. A deprecated field or value stays valid for one minor version.

Reader gate for 0.6. Readers before 0.6 do not read min_reader_version. So a release of format 0.6 is published only after SDK releases that read 0.6 are out: they leave out no_constants sets, know ok and flagged, and refuse a release whose min_reader_version is newer than they support. The release gate checks this before it publishes a release of format 0.6.

Converters

Converters, in both directions, with a lossless round trip as a release gate: TICON CSV, XTide TCD (through libtcd), and NOAA harmonic-constant JSON. The reference predictor publishes conformance vectors (expected high and low water times and heights at named stations and dates) so that other predictors can check their results.
