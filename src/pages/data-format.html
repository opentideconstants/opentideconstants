Data format

The fields of a release, its JSON Schema and the converters. The files and where to download them are on Downloads.

Fields

The format is 1.0 (format_version "1.0"). The JSON document has seven top-level fields: format_version, release, conventions, licences, constituents, astro_tables and stations. astro_tables is required when a convention has the nodal handling f_u_at_prediction.

station
A stable station_id in the OTC namespace (never changed, never reused), name, country, position, IANA time zone, type (reference or subordinate), aliases in other systems (NOAA, GESLA, TICON, XTide, Kartverket, Slackwater), and the recommended_set_id. An alias id belongs to one station only in a release. A station id that is not used any more stays as a tombstone: removed_reason is merged or duplicate, and merged_into names the station that holds its records. A station is never removed because its records are short, broken or from a source that failed a check. A reference station has at least one constant set, and its recommended_set_id is null only when every set is no_constants. A subordinate station has constant sets, subordinate_offsets or current_offsets.
constant set
One per source record: source, source type (official, gauge or model), quantity (water_level or current), record id and version, record span (start, end and hours of good data, good_hours, required on every set fitted from a gauge record), datum (see below), convention_id, licence_id, QC status and flags, the constituents, and provenance. Every record stays listed. A station can have several sets; one is recommended by a published rule. Only sets of the same quantity compete for it (water level or current). A short record never outranks a set at the same station that has constants and more hours of good data, and a set with no constants is never recommended.
datum
The vertical reference of a constant set, and of a subordinate station's offsets (subordinate_offsets.datum). zero says what the zero is: chart_datum, gauge_zero, vertical_datum (a national height system such as NAP, RH 2000, NN2000 or OD Malin), msl or unknown. zero_name is the source's name for it, for example MLLW, PNP or NAP. Both are optional. msl_offset_m is the mean level above the zero (m). named gives levels in metres above the same zero (negative when below), one value per key. A key starts with a lower-case letter, has only lower-case letters, digits and _, is at most 31 characters, and is not mean_level, zero or chart. basis is required with named and has one entry per key (see below). chart_datum is the key in named that is the chart datum, for example mllw, lat or tide_table_datum. When the station's chart-datum publisher publishes a chart datum, chart_datum names that published level. When it publishes none, chart_datum can name a computed or observed level, and its basis says which. A published chart datum is never replaced, per station: when any set of a station has one, no set of that station has a computed or observed chart datum. The published chart datum is its publisher's: the charting authority in the Baltic and the Kattegat, elsewhere the source that states it. A computed or observed level never replaces a published one: where a source publishes a level under a canonical key, an OTC value for the same datum has the key otc_ plus that key, for example otc_lat next to NOAA's lat, and an otc_ key is never of kind published.
datum basis
How one level was obtained. kind is published (given by the source, unchanged except for units and zero; method source), computed (from harmonic constants; method harmonic_extremes on a constant set, or subordinate_offsets for LAT and HAT at a subordinate station, from its reference station's predicted extremes with the offsets applied) or observed (reduced from a set's gauge record by NOAA's tidal-datum method; method first_reduction, standard, modified_range_ratio or direct). A subordinate station's datum has only source and subordinate_offsets levels. epoch is the period the level refers to. Every observed level from a comparison, and every 19-year determination, refers to the OTC datum epoch, exactly 2002-01-01 to 2020-12-31 (OTC 2002-2020). Computed LAT and HAT come from 19 years of predictions, 2020 to 2038 (OTC LAT 2020-2038). A published level has the source's epoch when the source states one for it, for example Kartverket's MSL (1996 to 2014). NOAA's LAT and HAT get none: NOAA computes them over a 40-year window, currently 2000 to 2040, not stated per station. data_span gives the gauge data an observed level comes from (first and last day, and the number of months). control names the control station of a comparison (station_id and set_id), or a published level's control station: the source's own id (source_id, required, for example the id in NOAA's ctrlStation) and its station_id when that station is in the release. uncertainty_m is one standard deviation in metres. Its uncertainty_basis is source for a published level (only when the source gives one), and calibrated or propagated for a computed or observed level, which always has one. Calibration uses OTC's cut-down experiments and comparisons with published or gauge-computed values, on the tuning partition and on gauges outside all partitions, never on a holdout or reserve gauge. OTC's validation stations are split by a fixed hash into three partitions: the tuning partition, used to set methods and thresholds, and the holdout and the reserve, kept apart so that validation is independent. A first_reduction without the flag no_qualified_control is a 19-year determination: the schema requires at least 216 months and the OTC epoch, and the release build checks that the months are inside the epoch and that each calendar month counts in at least 17 of the 19 years. A first reduction with the flag no_qualified_control is an average over the record's own months, with no suitable control station, from at most 228 months and never from a record with a 19-year determination's coverage: its epoch equals its data span, so it is not adjusted to the OTC epoch. The direct method goes with the flag truncated_lows (low waters are cut off), each with the other, and gives only high-water levels: mhw and mhhw (or otc_mhw, otc_mhhw). An observed level has the flag short_record exactly when it comes from under 12 months of data. Other flags: time_base_unverified, time_base_disputed, sampling_assumed, gaps, segment, short_record (for a computed level: constants from under one year of good data, which does not follow the IHO recommendation for computing LAT and HAT), microtidal and provisional (published levels only). Ranges (MN, GT, DHQ, DLQ) are not stored: they are differences of stored levels of the same kind and epoch. The release build checks what the schema cannot: basis and named have the same keys, every otc_ key has a published key next to it, a comparison's control resolves to a set with a 19-year determination over the same epoch of every control level that the method's formulas use, a published chart datum is never replaced at the station, an average over the record's own months has its epoch equal to its data span and covers whole years once it has 12 months, and every date is a real date with its period's start on or before its end.
QC status and flags
qc_status is ok (constants, no flags), flagged (constants, and qc_flags says what to know) or no_constants (the set is listed, but no constants were fitted; no_constants_reason is too_short or fit_unusable, and the set has at least one flag). No status marks a record as removed: every record stays listed. Every flag has a reason (required): time_base, broken_record, short_record, carried_over, microtidal, non_tidal_signal or sibling_disagreement. A flag's values holds the numbers behind the flag; its keys never name a status. A carried_over set keeps the constants of an earlier release, named in provenance.carried_from_release, because its source failed a check in this build.
provenance
Where the set came from and what was done to it. It is a closed list of keys, so no key can carry a status: qc_status is the only status of a set. For every source: adapter_version, input_sha256, tcd_sha256, selection_reason, decision (the release gate's tier, outcome, rule and reason), carried_from_release, and build (required on every set): the build-only fields build_commit (required), built_at, fetched_at, run_id and tool_versions, which change on every rebuild, so the release content digest leaves the whole object out. Source-specific keys are listed on the source pages: JMA (owner, redistribution, phase_reference_longitude_deg, jma_station_code, analysis_years, source_constituents_not_published) and LINZ (member, position, header_dropped). The JMA keys are valid only on a jma set and the LINZ keys only on a linz set. A JMA set in a release file always has owner jma and redistribution redistribute. A new key is added to the schema before a writer uses it.
time-base annotation
Required on every set fitted from a gauge record (time_base): the code (verified, corrected, unverified or disputed), a reason for people, the declared and published time base, the correction from and to (only when corrected), any months left out (months_dropped, a field, not a class), the build that decided it (decided_in, and optionally its decided_build_commit), and the evidence: per comparison, the comparator kind and id, its distance (km), the measured lag in minutes (the record's time stamp of an event minus the comparator's time of it, so an hourly mean stamped at the start of its hour is about −30), and an optional detail for people. A time base is a class such as utc_instant, utc_mean_start60, utc_plus_1, local_dst:Europe/Oslo or step:2010-03-01. An unverified or disputed time base also has a time_base flag. See Time-base checks.
broken record
record_issues, one per problem: datum_step, flatline, spikes, gaps, clock_step, quantised, out_of_range, unstable_constituents, neighbour_disagreement or no_tide, with where it is and why. fit_segment names the part the constants are fitted to; it is absent when the whole record is fitted. An issue's values holds the numbers behind it; its keys never name a status. Comes with a broken_record flag. See Broken-record checks.
short record
A short_record flag, the record length (record_span.good_hours), the constituents left out in dropped_constituents (at least one; a Rayleigh drop names the constituent it cannot be separated from, not_separable_from), and expected_accuracy: the length long records were cut to, how many, and the expected amplitude and phase error or the expected time and height error. A record with less than about 15 days of good data (release.fit_min_good_hours) is no_constants with too_short. See Short records.
constituent
OTC canonical name and the source's own name, Doodson number, speed (°/h), amplitude (m), Greenwich phase (°), uncertainties, and why it was kept. Dropped constituents are listed with the reason. Names are at most 15 characters.
constituent table
One row per constituent name and constituent-table version in the release: Doodson number, speed (°/h) and nodal formula id.
astronomical tables
astro_tables, one entry per table: its astro_table_id, V0 model, tables_sha256 (the SHA-256 of the XTide harmonics file whose values it equals), generator, first_year and last_year, and per constituent the speed (°/h) and two arrays with one value per year: v0u_deg, V0 at 1 January 00:00 UTC plus u at the middle of the year, in degrees rounded to 0.01 and reduced to [0, 360), and f, the node factor at the middle of the year, rounded to 0.0001. Each value holds for the whole year and steps at 00:00 UTC on 1 January. The keys of a row are speed_deg_per_hour, v0u_deg and f. A predictor takes V0+u and f from here. See Conventions.
current bin
Only in a set with quantity current, which has no water-level constituents. One per depth bin: bin number, depth (m) and how it is measured (below_surface, below_chart_datum or unknown, from NOAA's S, B and U), major-axis azimuth (° true; the minor axis is positive 90° clockwise from it), mean flood and ebb directions, the mean current along the major and minor axes (m/s, signed), and per constituent the speed (°/h), major- and minor-axis amplitude (m/s) and Greenwich phase (°). The station's default_current_bin names the bin to show first.
subordinate offsets
Reference station, time offsets at high and low water (minutes), height offsets, and whether they are ratios or additive, as NOAA gives them, and the station's own datum: the levels its source publishes for it, and LAT and HAT computed by the offsets, above the station's chart datum as the offsets define it (for NOAA, its MLLW). A subordinate current station has current_offsets instead, one per bin: reference station and bin, mean flood and ebb directions, time adjustments for maximum flood, slack before ebb, maximum ebb and slack before flood (minutes), and flood and ebb amplitude ratios.
convention
Phase reference, V0 model, nodal handling, constituent-table version, the astronomical table it uses (astro_table_id, required with f_u_at_prediction), and the result of the per-source check that confirmed it. See Conventions.
validation
Per station and window: time mean absolute error, 95th percentile, bias, height error, range error, missed and extra events, the reference source and its distance, and the same numbers for the previous release. See Validation.
release
The datestamp, build time, upstream source versions, build commit and changelog URL, and concept_doi: the one DOI for all versions. Cite a release by the concept DOI and its datestamp. fit_min_good_hours is the build's minimum hours of good data for a fit. A release file has no version DOI: Zenodo makes it after the files are fixed, so it is only in OTC_index.json and the latest pointers (zenodo_version_doi). min_reader_version is the oldest format version a reader must understand to use the release safely.
release index and latest pointers
OTC_index.json lists every release, newest first, in releases. OTC_latest.json and OTC_latest-f{MAJOR}.json are one entry each. An entry has the datestamp, format_version, min_reader_version, concept_doi, zenodo_version_doi (or null), content_sha256, and the name, URL, size and SHA-256 of each file. Optional: created, changes, source_versions, changelog_url and zenodo_material (pipeline only; true when the release gets its own Zenodo version at publish). A reader checks min_reader_version before it downloads a release.
licence
SPDX id (or a LicenseRef- id), provider, the citation the provider requires, if any, the assembled attribution text, and commercial_use. When commercial_use is false, every set with that licence_id is for non-commercial use only: restriction says why, and terms_url links the upstream terms. See Licence and attribution.

JSON Schema

  • otc-1.0.schema.json: the JSON Schema (draft 2020-12) for format_version 1.0. Each line of the .jsonl file validates against #/$defs/station, and the .meta.json file against #/$defs/meta.
  • otc-index-1.0.schema.json: the JSON Schema for OTC_index.json. Each latest pointer validates against #/$defs/release_info. index-example.json is an example.
  • example.json: a small example document with illustrative values (not data). Its astronomical table holds the real values of its eight constituents for 2024 to 2026. It has one tide station with two constant sets (a GESLA set with a 19-year determination of its observed levels, and a Kartverket set with published levels), one tombstone, one current station (NOAA ACT1616, with five of its constituents and its mean current converted from NOAA's published values), one subordinate current station, one station with five GESLA records (a corrected time base with observed levels reduced through a control station, a broken record fitted on its usable segment, a short record with a disputed time base, a record too short for constants, and a broken record with no usable segment), a NOAA-style tide station with published levels and a computed otc_lat, its subordinate station with published MLLW and LAT and HAT by offsets, an RWS gauge in NAP with an average over its own months, an SMHI gauge in RH 2000 whose chart datum is RH 2000, and an FMI gauge in N2000 whose chart datum is the older MSL-based one (mw). One of the fjord's GESLA records states its zero as the chart datum.

The format version is separate from the release date. A new optional field is a minor change. A removed or renamed field, or a changed unit or convention, is a major change. A change to an enum is a minor change for the schema, but it can change what a reader does with a set, so it raises min_reader_version. A deprecated field or value stays valid for one minor version.

Converters

Converters, in both directions, with a lossless round trip as a release gate: TICON CSV and NOAA harmonic-constant JSON. An XTide TCD file is not in the first release. It is planned as a one-way export, with other export formats, in a later release. The reference predictor publishes conformance vectors (expected high and low water times and heights at named stations and dates) so that other predictors can check their results.
