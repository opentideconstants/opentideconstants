About

Who runs it

OTC is developed and run by Jordan Ritter, a prolific software architect and serial entrepreneur. He is a lifelong contributor to open-source software, having authored ngrep, included in every major Linux distribution today, aimock, used by many AI developers to add LLM determinism to developing and testing AI products and services, and many other projects.

OpenTideConstants started as the data work behind webcaltides.org, a service that publishes tide and current predictions as calendar feeds. The webcaltides prediction engine is the reference predictor for OTC, and webcaltides uses OTC releases.

The automation keeps the data current without a person in the loop. Changes to the code and the rules are made in public, through the repository.

Report a problem or ask a question

Use the GitHub issue tracker. Issues are public, so others can see what was reported and how it was resolved. For a problem with a station, include the station_id, the release date, and what you compared it with.

Every automated decision in a release is stored in a public table with its evidence. If you think a decision is wrong, open an issue that names the station; a confirmed issue reverses the decision in the next release.

Licence and attribution

  • The constants OTC derives are published under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence (CC BY 4.0).
  • Derived constants only. OTC publishes harmonic constants fitted from sea-level records. It never redistributes the raw sea-level series. To get the raw data, download it from GESLA; OTC publishes a manifest of checksums so you can check your copy against the one the build used.
  • This follows the practice of TICON-4 (doi:10.17882/109129), which publishes constants derived from GESLA under CC BY 4.0.
  • NOAA constants and offsets are US Government data in the public domain (LicenseRef-PublicDomain-USGov). OTC publishes them unmodified and marks them as NOAA data. They are not presented as an official NOAA product.
  • Kartverket constants are published by Kartverket under CC BY 4.0. OTC converts their phases from UTC+1 to Greenwich and records the conversion.
  • LINZ constants are published by Toitū Te Whenua Land Information New Zealand under CC BY 4.0. OTC converts their phases to Greenwich and records the conversion.
  • JMA constants are published by the Japan Meteorological Agency under the Public Data License (Version 1.0), which JMA states is compatible with CC BY 4.0. OTC publishes only the stations JMA owns, converts their phases from the local meridian to Greenwich and records the conversion.
  • Every constant set carries its own licence_id and attribution text in the release files.

Attribution

When you use OTC data, credit OpenTideConstants (see How to cite) and the upstream providers of the stations you use:

GESLA
Constants fitted from GESLA records: acknowledge the GESLA website and cite Haigh et al. (2023) and Woodworth et al. (2017), as the GESLA downloads page asks. For records that come from UHSLC, also cite Caldwell et al. (2015).
Data providers
Each GESLA record comes from a national or regional agency, and each fitted set names its provider. The release files carry the full attribution text per set.
NOAA CO-OPS
Credit NOAA's Center for Operational Oceanographic Products and Services for NOAA constants and offsets.
Kartverket
Credit Kartverket (the Norwegian Mapping Authority) for Kartverket constants.
LINZ
Credit Toitū Te Whenua Land Information New Zealand, in text, for LINZ constants.
JMA
Cite the source, as the JMA terms require, and say that the content was edited: “Source: Japan Meteorological Agency (気象庁) website, tide tables (https://www.data.jma.go.jp/kaiyou/db/tide/suisan/). Edited by OpenTideConstants (units converted to metres, phase lags converted to Greenwich).”

The complete list of providers, with the citation text each one requires, is generated from the data by the release build. A release fails its build if a provider in the data is missing from this list.

Thanks

OTC depends on the work of the agencies that run tide gauges, the GESLA team that assembles their records, and the TICON team, whose constants and methods are the reference this project compares against.

How to cite

A citation lets a reader get the exact data you used. Each release has its own DOI, through Zenodo, and one concept DOI resolves to the latest release.

  • Cite the release you used by its date (OTC_{YYYYMMDD}) and its DOI. Both are in the release files and on Downloads.
  • To cite the project as a whole, use the concept DOI, or the CITATION.cff file that GitHub and reference managers read:
OpenTideConstants contributors. OpenTideConstants: open tidal harmonic constants.
{{site.url}}/

Also cite the upstream data of the stations you used, as listed under Attribution. GESLA asks for Haigh et al. (2023) and Woodworth et al. (2017), and Caldwell et al. (2015) for UHSLC records. Where you compare with TICON, cite the TICON release you used, for example TICON-4, doi:10.17882/109129.
